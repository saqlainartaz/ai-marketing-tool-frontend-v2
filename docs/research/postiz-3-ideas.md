# Ideas from reading Postiz

Not a feature wishlist. These are the things that changed my thinking about our product while
reading ~700 files of a mature competitor-adjacent codebase. Sorted by how much I'd argue for
them.

---

## The strategic read

Postiz is a **distribution** tool with AI bolted on. We are a **knowledge and governance** tool
with distribution not yet built. The gap between them is the whole product thesis, and reading
their code made it sharper rather than blurrier:

| | Postiz | Us |
|---|---|---|
| Where content comes from | your own past posts, by category | ingested brand knowledge, with provenance |
| Why this claim is safe | nothing | guardrails, softened before you see it |
| Who says yes | nobody — it just publishes | universal approval gate, undo on everything |
| Who the client is | a channel grouping called `Customer` | a first-class persona with their own door |
| Where it goes | 34 platforms | nowhere yet |

**They cannot easily add what we have. We can eventually add what they have.** Brand knowledge
with provenance is a data and trust problem that takes a year; platform integrations are a
grind that takes weeks per platform and can be bought, borrowed or deferred. That asymmetry is
the strongest argument I've seen for our current sequencing.

It also means the "we're behind because we can't post yet" instinct is wrong. Posting is the
commodity end of this market.

---

## 1. Steal the pending-publish contract before we write any publishing code

Covered in the reuse report, but it belongs here too because it's the one idea I'd act on
regardless of everything else.

Our product's entire promise is *nothing goes out without your yes*. The failure mode that
breaks that promise isn't publishing without approval — we've designed that out. It's
**publishing twice** because a request timed out and we retried. Postiz solves this with a
three-state contract and `maximumAttempts: 1` on irreversible mutations.

We should design our publish path around that invariant from day one, and we should add
"published twice" to the list of things `publishedWithoutApprovalCount()` is really claiming.

## 2. Per-platform rules belong in the generation context, not just the validator

Their `@Rules('...')` decorator attaches natural-language platform constraints to a provider,
and those get injected into the AI's context *before* it writes. So the model knows LinkedIn
comments can't hold media, that an array means a thread on X but a first comment on LinkedIn.

We currently guardrail *after* generation — we soften a claim once it exists. Rules-before-
generation is the same idea one step earlier and much cheaper. Our engine already has the
guardrail machinery; this is pointing it at platform constraints as well as brand ones.

Concretely: a `platform_rules` table alongside our brand rules, injected into the prompt, and
surfaced in the UI as the same honey "protection" primitive we already have.

## 3. A public share link is a weak version of something we should do properly

Their client-feedback loop is `/p/:id` — a public page showing the post with a comment thread.
No approve action, no state change, no notification. It is, frankly, a hack.

But the *shape* is right and we have the missing half. We already have:
- a client identity and a magic-link door
- an approval gate with a recorded decision
- undo
- the handoff sheet with "share review link" (which currently points at `/review/:id`, **a route
  that does not exist** — worth fixing or removing)

A real client-review link — no login, shows the prepared object with its provenance and
guardrails, one Approve and one "not this one", the decision recorded — would be a genuinely
differentiated feature and it's mostly assembly of parts we've built. It's also the natural
answer for the persona who never logs in.

## 4. The creation-method badge is a provenance idea we should generalise

Every Postiz post records whether it came from the web app, the API, the AI agent, autopost or
the CLI, and the calendar shows a badge for it.

We care about provenance more than they do. "This draft came from your March episode at 18:40"
is our whole pitch — but we don't currently record *how the draft was made*: prepared for you,
written from your note, picked from an idea, or generated by the agent. That's cheap to add to
the content store and it makes the Library a real audit trail rather than a list.

## 5. Time slots are a better model than dates for a rhythm-based product

Postiz gives each channel a set of posting times and a `find-slot` endpoint that returns the
next free one. Everything — new post, duplicate, AI generation, autopost — routes through it.

Our Plan already promises a rhythm ("2 pieces a week · Tue, Thu"). But our content carries a
weekday key that always resolves into the current week, so the rhythm is decorative. Slots
would make the plan and the calendar the same object, which is what the Plan page claims to be.

This is the honest version of the "monthly push" feature I cut — and it's a smaller change.

## 6. Two things I'd deliberately do the opposite way

**Their onboarding is "connect channels, watch a video".** Ours is five questions that produce
a plan. Reading theirs made me more confident ours is right: a client who has connected five
channels and watched a video has given us nothing to write from. Don't let "reduce onboarding
friction" pressure erode the questions — they *are* the product's input.

**Their composer is a 704-line two-pane modal with 34 settings panels.** It's impressive and
it's exactly the "generic tool everyone can build" that we rejected. Our one-decision card is
the harder, better answer for our personas. The place their model wins is the *advanced user*
who wants to tune per-platform settings — and our answer to that should stay the work-mode
dial, not a settings wall.

## 7. Smaller things worth logging

- **Alt text and custom video thumbnails per media item.** Accessibility and quality; we have
  neither. The frame-grab-at-a-timestamp interaction is a nice touch.
- **Streaming named generation steps.** Our `AssemblyMoment` fakes this with a timer. Theirs
  streams real LangGraph node names. When our engine does real work, stream the real steps —
  the honesty is the point, and we already wrote that in `docs/UX_RULES.md`.
- **`integration-settings/:id` returning rules + limits + tools in an LLM-shaped payload.** A
  good pattern for our own BFF: one endpoint that tells the model what it's allowed to do.
- **Recurring computed at read time**, not materialised. Avoids a whole class of drift bugs.
- **A debug-payload copy action** for support (theirs is super-admin only). Would help our team
  reproduce a client's card exactly.
- **`refreshNeeded` as a first-class channel state** with a "reconnect this channel" flow.
  We'll need it the moment we hold platform tokens.

## 8. What reading this did NOT change

I went in expecting to find that we were behind. We aren't — we're solving a different and
harder problem, and the things we've built (provenance you can tap, guardrails with a diff, a
universal approval gate, undo on everything, four themes, WCAG 2.2 AA, a tested design system)
have **no counterpart in Postiz at all**. Their repo has zero tests and 182 `@ts-ignore`s.

The one place they're unambiguously ahead is the thing we deliberately haven't built yet.
That's a sequencing difference, not a quality gap — and after reading how much accumulated
platform trivia lives in those 34 providers, deferring it looks like the right call rather
than a delay.
