## Phase 1 — Design audit (morning)

You asked for a proper audit before any redesign, explicitly not a polish pass.

**What I did:** built a Playwright screenshot harness and captured 30+ shots
across mobile (390×844) and desktop (1366×850), every screen, every state, all
four themes. Audited against the spec's own rules and standard UI/UX priorities.

**Verdict: the bones were right, the skin was amateur.** The product thesis
survived — one decision per card, consequence on buttons, provenance,
guardrails. The execution undermined trust at exactly the moments the product
exists to build it.

**Critical findings:**

| # | Finding | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A text-rendering bug fractured draft copy — orphaned punctuation, broken mid-sentence — on the one surface where the client judges whether we're careful with their words | P0 |
| 2 | Emoji used as system icons throughout — colour-inconsistent across devices, the single biggest "rough sketch" signal | High |
| 3 | Surfaces washed out: grey cards on white with faint borders read as an unshipped wireframe | High |
| 4 | Stadium-pill buttons and blobby radii made the primary action look like a toy | High |
| 5 | Mobile screens collapsing into voids — 40–70% empty viewport with the action stranded at the bottom | High |
| 6 | Theme switcher overflowed the sidebar in every theme; "Night" label unreadable while in Night | P0 |

None of it was architectural, which was the good news — the fix was surgical.

## Phase 2 — "Desk", the design rehaul

You said you didn't like the design and authorised a complete rehaul. The
diagnosis was that it had no point of view — it read as a generic CRM, which
was the one thing you'd said it must not be.

**The system I built:** a tinted page canvas, raised white cards as objects,
inset wells for input — a desk with prepared work laid on it.

- **Colour as budget** — `--clay` is the one action, `--moss` marks wins,
  `--honey` marks protection. Nothing else gets colour. One accent-filled
  element per screen.
- **Type has three voices** — Bricolage Grotesque for moments, Inter for
  content and actions, JetBrains Mono for metadata.
- **Icons are drawn, never typed** — every emoji replaced with Lucide icons at
  one stroke weight.
- **Radius says what things are** — cards 16px, buttons 12px, chips full.
- **A void is a design failure** — every viewport composition either centres
  the moment or fills the space with meaning.

**Fixed in this phase:** the P0 text-flow bug (a real `<button>` is an atomic
inline-block, so it fractured wrapped sentences — rebuilt as an inline span
with a keyboard handler, plus a regression test); the sidebar overflow; button
width now matching the object it commits; contrast lifted to pass AA in all
four themes.

**Made durable:** 36 automated contrast checks across the four themes — one of
which caught a real failure — plus a text-flow regression test.

## Phase 4 — UX principles pass (evening)

You flagged that the experience wasn't being shaped by strong UX principles,
and asked me to research current best practice and apply it rather than
guessing.

**Research:** primary sources only — Nielsen Norman Group, GOV.UK Design System
and Service Manual, Stripe, IBM Carbon, Material Design 3, Atlassian, Intercom,
Monzo, and W3C/WCAG 2.2. Then audited every screen against it.

### What was structurally missing

**Feedback and reversibility.** Approving a draft changed the screen and said
nothing. Now every decision announces itself and offers **Undo for 8 seconds** —
approve, skip, and mark-as-posted are all reversible. I chose act-then-undo over
"are you sure?" dialogs deliberately: nothing in this product can publish, so
there's no irreversible action worth interrupting anyone for.

**Three states that didn't exist at all:** no error page, no not-found page, no
loading state anywhere. All three are now designed. The error page leads with
the promise that matters when something breaks — nothing was sent, nothing was
lost.

**Buttons that didn't name their object.** "Good to go" became "Approve post" —
and "Approve reply" on a review card, "Approve email" on an email. "Not this
one" became "Skip post". "Toss it" became "Discard draft". Wizards advance with
"Continue", not "Next".

**Disabled buttons that explained nothing.** A greyed-out Continue tells you
you're stuck without telling you why. Onboarding and the create flow now keep it
live and say what's missing when pressed.

**A failure styled as a success.** A clipboard error rendered in a green box
with a tick. Fixed.

**Two copy problems doing real damage:** the Workspace headline read "The door
in the back" — a metaphor only we could decode, now "Ask for anything."
Onboarding showed two progress counters with different denominators ("02/6"
beside "Question 1 of 4"), making users do arithmetic to work out where they
were.

### Accessibility

Target is WCAG 2.2 AA — what every current regulation actually references.

- Skip link, and focus moves to the main region on every route change
- The phone overflow sheet is a real dialog — Escape closes it, focus returns
  to the trigger
- Calendar marks meet the 24px minimum target size and carry real names
  instead of hover-only tooltips (a tooltip never appears on touch)
- Week ribbon dots no longer convey status by colour alone
- Button consequence lines moved to `aria-describedby`, so a speech-input user
  can say exactly what they see
- Form validation properly announced and associated

Two findings came from the tests rather than from reading the code: the new
target-size gate caught a 16px link on Today, and the onboarding screens turned
out to have no `<main>` landmark at all.

### Written down

`docs/UX_RULES.md` — every rule with the source that justifies it, plus a
seven-question check to run against any screen. So the next round starts from
these instead of rediscovering them.
